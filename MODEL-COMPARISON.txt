WHY GRADIENT WAS SELECTED
ModernBERT produced substantially more human false alarms, so it was rejected.
Gradient reduced wrong answers in both selection sets and the separate batch.
Its larger uncertainty on creative writing is explicitly retained and reported.
The initial selection rule asked for no loss of correct answers in either set.
After selection results, this was revised to favour fewer wrong answers even
when uncertainty rises. That amendment was recorded BEFORE confirmation-batch
inference. It was not a fully preregistered experiment. Thresholds were unchanged.

TEST LIMITS
Small convenience samples, not a random or representative survey of writing.
HC3 contains older ChatGPT responses; overlap with detector training is possible.
Historical human text may also have been seen during model training.
Fresh AI examples came from one assistant, not multiple independent generators.
Human examples span older books, not modern emails or student assignments.
The separate batch was held out from our selection, not proven absent from the
model's training data. No claim of statistical significance is made.
The tool still supports English prose only, with at least 100 words.
It estimates writing patterns; it cannot prove who wrote a document.

IMPLEMENTATION
Gradient uses its own tokenizer and [CLS]/[SEP] tokens (IDs 1 and 2).
Its one output logit is passed through a stable sigmoid. A single-output
softmax would incorrectly return 100% for every input, and is not used.
Weight, tokenizer and config hashes are verified before loading.
Balanced windows of up to 480 body tokens use the same existing 20/80 decision
cutoffs, token-weighted mean and passage-disagreement rule. No tuning occurred.
Scores remain model signals, not authorship probabilities or AI-word percentages.
Hidden-character checks stay separate. Original files and old reports unchanged.
