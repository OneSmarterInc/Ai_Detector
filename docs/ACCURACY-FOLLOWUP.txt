AI File Inspector: harder accuracy check, 4 November 2026

OUTCOME
No tested scoring change demonstrated the required improvement. Gradient Q4
and the current 20/80 cutoffs are retained. Do not claim a new model accuracy
gain from this work. Two text-encoding bugs were fixed separately.

WHAT WAS TESTED
96 Beemo texts: 32 human originals, 32 original AI outputs and 32 AI outputs
edited by human experts. Four categories: generation, open questions, rewriting
and summarization. All ten source AI generators are represented.
Half was used for development. The other half used different prompt IDs and
was not inspected to choose cutoffs or combinations.

Current Gradient results across the 96 texts:
Origin                         Correct   Wrong   Not sure   Total
Human                            20        7        5        32
Original AI                      28        2        2        32
Expert-edited AI                  21        6        5        32
Total                            69       15       12        96

Development set: 36 correct, 6 wrong, 6 unsure (48 texts).
Separate confirmation: 33 correct, 9 wrong, 6 unsure (48 texts).
Expert-edited AI was evaluated as AI-origin, not entirely AI-written. Its human
style can be legitimate. The detector cannot establish whether AI was ever
involved in producing a text.

TRIED AND REJECTED
1. Retaining paragraph/line breaks: all 96 examples produced exactly the same
   token IDs after the model's tokenizer normalized whitespace. No benefit.
2. 25 cutoff combinations for each cleanup method, chosen on development only:
   none reduced wrong decisions while retaining correct decisions and avoiding
   extra human false alarms. No changes were tuned on confirmation results.
3. Five weighted combinations with the previous RoBERTa model, on development
   only: some reduced wrong answers, but produced substantially more uncertainty
   and fewer correct answers. None met the acceptance rules. No ensemble shipped.
   This additional hypothesis was tested after the cutoff experiment failed,
   but before confirmation scores were inspected.

FIXES
A character-encoding error split curly-apostrophe words such as "we're" (with
a curly apostrophe) into two words. That could allow fewer than 100 real words
to be scored. The word count now treats these contractions as one word.
The same encoding problem prevented a passage-disagreement result from matching
its specific plain-English report explanation. That match is restored.
The desktop progress ellipsis was also repaired.
These are correctness fixes, not evidence of better authorship classification.
All 70 application tests passed, including the two new regression tests,
offline model inference, file extraction and native desktop behavior.

LIMITS
This harder sample is small and intentionally selects texts between 110 and
550 regex word tokens. It is not representative of all writing or current AI.
Scores are signals, not probabilities of authorship. The model authors report
evaluating Beemo; absence from training has not been independently verified.
The earlier 138-example comparison remains a separate sample-specific result.
Do not combine the two studies into a universal accuracy rating.

of row ID. All three origins remain together; all exact texts were distinct.

local evaluation workspace and are not included in shared reports or training.
Source licensing differs by field; see the dataset card. No training was done.

evaluation scripts, scores, selection records and regression-test results.

NEXT SUBSTANTIAL ACCURACY WORK
Evaluate or train a model on a much larger, correctly labelled collection that
includes modern AI generators, real human edits, ordinary human writing and
non-native English. Keep whole prompt/source families out of training for final
testing. A successful upgrade must reduce mistakes without hiding the cost in
Not sure results. More models or stricter score cutoffs alone are not evidence
of better accuracy.
